# DFMEA (stage 3)

Built with AI assistance (Claude). A design failure mode and effects analysis of my own research PCB, the SRAM-PUF shield (see [DFM_REVIEW.md](DFM_REVIEW.md) for the manufacturing side). This is a self-directed desk exercise.
No team scored it, there is no field data, and it is not industry experience. The table is data (`dfmea/puf_shield_dfmea.csv`); `python -m pufdfm dfmea dfmea/puf_shield_dfmea.csv` checks and ranks it, and the table below is generated by the same command.

## What the board does

Two 23K640 serial SRAMs (U1, U2) sit in sockets on a shield that plugs onto a Feather M0 or an STM32 board. Their ground pins are joined to one switched node (`/SW_GND`) that a 2N7000 (Q1) connects to ground, so the host can
cut the SRAM supply and read the start-up pattern. The host's gate pin reaches Q1 through R1; R2 is a pull-down on the gate. SPI and chip-select lines come from the host. A 4.7 kOhm resistor (R3) is the pull-up for an optional DS18B20 on J3.

## How the scores were set

Every score is my judgement from one design and one or two builds. Each row says what kind of evidence stands behind it:

| Evidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| observed | something that happened on this board (from my own notes of the work) |
| photo | visible in the photos of the assembled board |
| thesis | a result in my thesis |
| design analysis | read from the schematic or PCB, or measured from the layout with this repository's tool |
| judgement | general engineering risk; nothing on this board shows it |

| Score | Severity (effect on this rig) | Occurrence (my judgement) | Detection (before it affects results) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | no effect | not expected | certain to be caught |
| 2 to 3 | cosmetic or a small rework | unlikely | caught by a routine check |
| 4 to 5 | one capture or session lost, noticed at once | plausible; seen elsewhere | caught only by a specific test |
| 6 to 7 | board unusable, or wrong data that may be used | seen on this board | hard to notice |
| 8 | a chip or the host board is damaged | seen repeatedly | very hard to notice |
| 9 to 10 | safety hazard (not expected here) | certain | not detectable |

The risk priority number (RPN) is severity x occurrence x detection. It is simple and it has known problems: different score combinations give the same number, and a high-severity, rarely-occurring failure can rank below a trivial one. Read the severity column on its own as well.

## Reading it

- **Observed events rank where the evidence puts them.** The Q1 solder bridge (D01) is the highest-scoring failure that actually happened on this board. Its cause is geometric (a 0.22 mm copper gap), which is why the Rev B change list starts with Q1's footprint.
- **The two highest numbers are not the biggest worries.** D07 (poor joints) scores high because it is likely and hard to spot; D10 (ESD) scores high because it can never be detected, and nothing on the board shows it. Both are cheap to reduce by working habits, not by redesign.
- **D03 is probably the cause of the first symptom in August.** R1 is the only connection between the host's gate pin and Q1 on this PCB, the build plan left R1 empty, the host pin toggled but Q1's gate did not, and later the gate leg read as floating. None of that is proof, and R1 was not among the suspects checked at the time. A two-step continuity test confirms or rules it out (see the action).
- **D18 matters for any future test.** With the switch on the ground side, the SRAM's supply pin stays at 3.3 V against board ground by design, so a flat 3.3 V on pin 8 says nothing about whether the switch works. The two captures that differed by only 0.08% are the better evidence, and they fit either an always-on or a never-powered chip.
- **Scores that would change with a measurement:** D05 (measure the SRAM supply during power-down on the shield), D04 (probe the gate across a reset), D01 and D02 (how the second Q1 fault was resolved).

## What this analysis did not do

No fault injection, no testing of any failure mode on purpose, no thermal or electrical simulation, no review of the firmware's behaviour at reset, and no comparison with a second built board.
